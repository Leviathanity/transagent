1. Purpose
To accomplish our world-class quality vision, Ceer is committed to forming a strong partnership with its
supplier base. To achieve the expectations of Ceer and ��Our Customers�� the suppliers shall establish and
maintain a comprehensive Quality Operating System, to assure compliance with the requirements of the
contract and this document.
The Purpose of this Supplier Quality Handbook is to outline the minimum quality requirements for the
Suppliers of Ceer in accordance with IATF 16949. The suppliers must carry out Advanced Product Quality
Planning (APQP) and Production Part Approval Process (PPAP) for all series production, as outlined in
IATF 16949. Suppliers not having achieved IATF16949 must submit a waiver form and plan to acquire the
certification that will be used in the sourcing process.
Unless otherwise specified in the following, the requirements outlined in this process are orientated towards
the most recent standards as outlined by the Automotive Industry Action Group (AIAG), specific to the
Production Part Approval Process (PPAP) Manual and Advanced Product Quality Planning (APQP) Manual
3rd Edition & Control Plan 1st Edition released by AIAG.
This Supplier Quality Handbook, however, is not intended to change specific requirements, which in case
of inconsistencies, the AIAG standards shall take precedence.
2. Scope
This handbook applies to all suppliers, who must ensure that the Ceer requirements, for all vehicle
subsystems, are understood and implemented along the whole Supply Chain.
These requirements are designed to assure that all products (systems, modules, components, bulk materials,
etc.) supplied to Ceer meet all known drawings, standards, specifications and agreements. Meeting these
minimum requirements will ensure customer satisfaction.
The term ��shall��, as used in this document, indicates mandatory requirements. The word ��should�� indicate
a suggestion or recommendation.

4. Responsibilities
It is the responsibility of the supplier to ensure all the required products are delivered on time, to the
specified quantity and in conformance with the quality, performance, and engineering specifications.
The supplier shall communicate with Ceer if any supply chain delivery, quality, or performance issues arise,
and take adequate countermeasures to contain and mitigate such issues.
Suppliers shall leverage their expertise and support Ceer by providing feedback, highlighting concerns,
Things Gone Wrong (TGW), sharing lessons learned, and suggested improvement actions in order to
enhance overall quality, reduce cost, scrap, and improve throughput, among other.
As early as possible in the program, the Supplier Senior Management shall:
? Communicate to Ceer their current organizational structure and individual contacts.
? Define the individuals responsible for carrying out Advanced Product Quality Planning (APQP).
The designated individuals performing those functions shall have sufficient expertise, well-defined
responsibility, authority, and organizational freedom to identify, evaluate and resolve quality problems that
may arise.
The Supplier Senior Management shall communicate the above requirements to Ceer by completing and
returning the Supplier Contact & Open Issues Tracking Program Specific (CEER-SQ-QM-001-F003) form.
Any changes to the organizational structure and APQP core team shall be communicated to the Ceer
Purchasing Buyer and Supplier Quality Specialist.
The official language for communication is English unless specified and agreed mutually by Ceer and
supplier. All documents shall be provided in English and shall refer to the metric system, except otherwise
stipulated in the Handbook.
The supplier is responsible for ensuring the availability of personnel who can communicate effectively in
English to facilitate efficient communication regarding all technical, quality, or any other APQP-related
topics.

5. Product Development Process
Ceer Product Development follows a series of processes/procedures defined as the Ceer Supplier
Development Process. This details the methods and tools used by Supplier Quality from the initial
assessment as a potential new supplier / location through launch and into serial production with tactical
monitoring within operations.
APQP framework exhibits standardized quality requirements, which enables suppliers to design, develop
and produce a product that meets Ceer requirements.
Fig.01. Ceer APQP Process Definition
Ceer APQP is a synchronization of product and process development activities and ensures communication
internally and externally.
The process of Advanced Product Quality Planning (APQP) at Ceer involves four key events, each
contributing to the successful development and launch of a product. These events are organized in a
sequential order: KO, SV1, SV2, and SV3.
KO (APQP Kick-off - Supplier Industrialization Planning):
The journey begins with the APQP Kick-off, a crucial milestone in the product development process. During
this stage, teams gather to initiate the project and set the groundwork for its success.
Supplier Industrialization Planning takes place, focusing on how suppliers will integrate into the production
process seamlessly.
SV1 (Product and Process Development - Design Freeze, and Hard Tool Kick-off):
SV1 represents a critical phase where product and process development are undertaken. Teams work
together to refine the product design and align it with production capabilities. The stage culminates with the
important milestones of Design Freeze and the initiation of Hard Tool manufacturing, marking a significant
step forward.

SV2 (Product and Process Validation - Produce Off Tool Parts, and Complete Validations):
SV2 focuses on validating the product and production processes. Off-tool parts are manufactured to ensure
they meet quality standards. Comprehensive validations are carried out to confirm that the product and
process are ready for serial production.
SV3 (PPAP Approval - Process Capability Verified, and PSW Approved):
The final stage, SV3, involves the submission of the Production Part Approval Process (PPAP) for approval.
Process Capability is thoroughly verified to ensure it meets required standards. The Production Part
Submission Warrant (PSW) is approved, indicating that the product is ready for full-scale production.
These four APQP events are strategically aligned to guide the product development process from inception
to production, with a strong emphasis on quality assurance and collaboration among teams and suppliers.
6. International Material Data System (IMDS)
To ensure compliance with the various legal and market requirements, Ceer requires its Suppliers to report
material and substance information for all types of purchased materials, components or items supplied to
Ceer. All substances and/or materials shall be reported to Ceer using the International Material Data System
(IMDS) (www.mdsystem.com).
Suppliers shall submit the required IMDS to Ceer as soon as possible upon award of new business, but
always as part of the PPAP process. The supplier IMDS information shall be subject to Ceer review and
approval. Once approved by Ceer, the Supplier of the material or component shall indicate such approval
in the PPAP documentation supplied to Ceer regardless of submission level requested.
The supplier shall also implement procedures or controls necessary to prevent the introduction of prohibited
and restricted substances in materials as specified herein into the final product and/or component supplied
to Ceer.
7. Ceer Requirement
Ceer supply management requirements are based on four key processes: supplier selection, new product
launch, continuous improvement, and supplier rapid issue containment and resolution. These key processes
are global in nature as are any of the referenced procedures.
I. Criteria for selection as a Ceer Supplier
i. IATF 16949 Registration
The goal for all Ceer suppliers of materials and services affecting production material is to demonstrate
compliance to IATF 16949. Suppliers shall also comply with Ceer specific requirements defined in the
Ceer Supplier Quality Handbook (CEER-QUSQ-SP5-L2-001).
Suppliers to Ceer shall have a plan to achieve conformity to IATF 16949. Unless otherwise specified,
conformity is demonstrated by third party certification to ISO 9001 (at minimum) or IATF 16949. This
is consistent with the expectations of Ceer business system that complies with IATF 16949
requirements. The scope of the requirement affects subassembly, sequencing, sorting, re-work and
calibration services in addition to direct material suppliers.

8. Advanced Product Quality Planning (APQP)
I. Introduction
Ceer uses Advanced Product Quality Planning (APQP) as Quality Project Management Process.
It enables Ceer to monitor a Supplier��s progress of component supply from Sourcing through to Production.
Ceer APQP process (CEER-QUSQ-SP5-L2-002) contains a set of actions and activities, which when
completed will ensure component delivery to Ceer for specific build phases, with the correct component(s)
maturity and installed manufacturing capacity.
APQP will be undertaken during all Projects by Ceer with all Suppliers that have been successfully sourced
for a new Project. It may also be used to manage change content in Model Year Updates for current
production Series.
APQP commences with the supplier within 30 days of Ceer Purchasing nomination and continues +90 days
of SORP.
APQP will continue until the Supplier has provided 100% of component(s) to Full PPAP Maturity to Ceer.
During the duration of the Project, a Core Team of people must be assembled comprising of various
members of different departments from Ceer and the Supplier.
The Core Team will normally comprise of:
? Ceer SQ Specialist
? Ceer Buyer
? Ceer Project Engineer
? Ceer Supply Chain Engineer (Logistics/Packaging)
? Supplier��s nominated representatives (Project Manager; Quality; Engineering; Logistics)
The APQP Process contains several steps which will be defined below.

9. Quality Activities Prior to Production Part Approval
I. Technical Reviews
Lead by the Ceer Engineer, this activity reviews the supplier��s technical proposal, to ensure that all
requirements in the RFQ package have been understood and the supplier has a plan to produce parts meeting
the Ceer expectations. The aim of this review is to identify capable suppliers which meet the Ceer
expectations.
Both the Ceer Product Development Engineer and Supplier Quality Specialist should identify agenda items
to be reviewed in the technical meeting and asses the supplier��s technical, manufacturing and quality
capabilities for continuation in the sourcing process.
The supplier��s understanding of all part and process specific requirements is assessed, to ensure these have
been considered in its proposal.
II. Sourcing Eligibility
The SQS carries out the Potential Supplier Assessment (PSA) activity, to determine if a supplier��s
manufacturing location is eligible to receive business from Ceer.
Upon the PSA activity completion, the SQS ensures that Supplier Development plans are created for all
potential suppliers where gaps are identified. It is the supplier��s responsibility to create these plans and
complete all improvement actions at the earliest time possible.
The SQS shall verify the progress periodically and drive the supplier to complete all activities before PPAP
submission / approval.
III. APQP Kick-off Meeting and Supplier Verification (SV)
The APQP Kick-Off meeting shall be led by the Ceer SQS and subsequent APQP meetings shall be held
on a regular basis, on a minimum cadence of one per month, and led by the supplier.
The APQP Kick-off meeting activities shall ensure as minimum, the following:
? Confirm APQP Core team members, substitutes, and escalation contacts.
? Determine meeting cadence and locations.
? Review Supplier Development Plan.
? Review current Open Issues List and document any additional points.
? Confirm volume and daily contracted capacity.
? Review Program Timing and milestones.
? Review quality deliverables for all major Program Milestones.
? Review gauges, tooling, and equipment requirements.
? Agree on supplier verification (SV) audit timing based on Program Milestones

The supplier shall create and keep updated APQP timing charts and Open Issues List. These documents will

VI. Design Reviews
Reviews to ensure the design has been defined to enable construction of tools and gauges.
Design owner schedules and conducts workshops to define the critical product and process control
characteristics.
The design owner will also schedule and conduct GD&T reviews to define the dimensioning and tolerances
on the drawing (3D models).
The SQS and supplier are supporting these reviews to:
? Buy-off the GD&T structure.
? Agree which special characteristics can be measured with attribute gauges.
? Confirm if the current manufacturing process can achieve the specified tolerances.
Any concerns relative to the special characteristics or GD&T or manufacturability of the part need to be
documented on the APQP open issues list by the supplier.
The supplier shall ensure all special characteristics, GD&T or product and process specific requirements
are included in the tooling, gauges and equipment.
VII. Gauges, Tooling, and Equipment Review and Approval
The gauge, tooling and equipment reviews start at the APQP Kick-off meeting. The purpose of these reviews
is to ensure the manufacturing process is designed to the latest part engineering level and it is capable of
producing parts to the quality and rate required by Ceer Program requirements as follows:
The APQP Team, led by the SQS, will review:
? Tooling; gauges and testing equipment concepts.
? Component functional testing concept and criteria.
? Matching Build strategy, requirements, and data collection.
This is to ensure that the supplier manufacturing process can meet component design intent and required
process capability. Tooling and equipment reviews will be held throughout the building process as part of
the APQP meetings and Supplier Verification (SV).
The supplier is required to ensure all tooling, gauge and equipment is designed to the latest Ceer engineering
design level.
A detailed timing chart shall be submitted by the supplier for all tooling, gauges and equipment. At
minimum, the supplier shall consider the following activities within their timing plan:
? Ceer Engineering Release.
? Manufacturing feasibility assessment.
? Tools and gauges concept and design finalization.
? RM ordering & arrival at tool maker for milling.
? Tools & Gauges KO at tool maker.
? Assy line KO at production supplier location (If any assy. process required).
? Tools and gauges construction and try-out (at tool maker)

VIII. Process Flow Charts
Upon sourcing completion and APQP Kick-off, the supplier shall define a production Process Flow Chart.
The production process flow chart shall include all items part of the manufacturing process, respecting the
nomenclature (move, inspect, store, etc.)
The supplier is expected to complete this activity as per APQP timing plan and submit the process flow
chart to SQS for review, including a completed checklist A-6, found in the AIAG Advanced Product
Quality Planning and Control Plan manual, Appendix A, Process Flow Chart Checklist A-6.

and Control plan and walk the manufacturing line to ensure all steps are completed. The supplier shall
initiate PFMEA team meeting, prior to sourcing, and involve SQS, Ceer Engineer, Manufacturing Engineer,
to provide a preliminary PFMEA.

13. Lot Traceability
All suppliers to Ceer shall have an effective lot definition and traceability procedure. The shipping number
shall be linked to the lot traceability procedure in such a way that the delivered product, unless otherwise
approved in writing by Ceer SQS, can be traced back to the raw material.
The maximum size of a lot shall consist of one shift or eight hours of production, whichever is smaller. The
lot definition shall reflect all significant processes influencing the component/material with the shipping lot
number reflecting the last value-added operation.
Suppliers shall ensure that their lot traceability system maintains its integrity throughout the entire extended
supply chain, including raw material and purchased components/products.
The lifecycle of many components begins and ends within the facility of the supplier. There are those
components, however, that require processing by outside companies to finish the process stream. These may
include heat treatment, forging, coining, stamping, machining, coating, and other various special processes.
If the original lot were batch processed through the different secondary processes, there would be no need
to change the original lot number. However, if the batch is split at a secondary processor, then the lot number
for each of the batches should be unique.
Once manufacturing/assembly begins, a lot number changes if:
? One shift of production or eight hours is reached.
? A new lot of raw material is being used.
? The components undergo another value-added process, and the original lot is divided during
processing.
? The lot number changes on any one of the components being used.
? When required, the supplier may need to implement:
o Serialized (maintains a one-to-one relationship between the finished goods�� serial
number and the components�� serial number) lot traceability; or
o Specific Lot (maintains a one-to-one relationship between the finished good serial
number and the components�� lot numbers) traceability for certain programs.
To clarify the difference between this and general traceability, consider a supplier who stamps a given
component. After stamping, two fasteners are then pressed into the stamping. General traceability is where
there is no lot traceability between the stamped component and the assembled parts. Specific traceability
would be where the lot numbers of the assembled components are traceable through the lot number of the
stamped component.

27. Executive Escalation (EE) Process
Suppliers providing Ceer with products and services that do not meet quality, delivery or planning
commitments and requirements are subject to enrollment in the escalation process.
Executive Escalation (EE) is initiated by Ceer. The purpose of EE is to ensure continuous improvement
of suppliers occurs with the best support of appropriate mentors.
EE classifies by three levels. In all levels, the Supplier takes responsibility to adapt Ceer requirement of
data, evidence and information sharing to exit EE.
EE Level C (EE-C): Triggered by the supplier chronic and repeated poor performance such as non-
conforming parts and delivery issues. Ceer will utilize a scorecard system to monitor supplier
performance (Under Development).
EE Level B (EE-B): Triggered by the failure of EE-C exiting, and/or quality/delivery performance with
continuing degradation.
EE Level A (EE-A): Triggered by the failure of EE-B exiting, and/or impact on vehicle launch and/or
continuity of delivery quality.
In all stages of EE, the supplier can exit with adapting of the following exit criteria:
? Agreement reached to resolve issue.
? Three to six months improvement glide path.
? Monitoring and follow up plan for recurrence.
Ceer responsible SQS plays a role of leading suppliers in this activity and responsible for:
? Escalation Facilitator for successful event.
? Prepare the Supplier for success with pre-meeting guidance and slide deck review.
? Monitoring and meeting on Exit Criteria providing Executive Reports on progress.
? Prepare the Supplier De-escalation to Exit.

32. Appendix D: Accessories
Definition
Additional components or features installed in a vehicle to enhance its functionality, comfort, safety,
performance, or aesthetics.
Marketing enabler to provide customized variants /upgrade options to improve the overall customer
experience and pride of ownership.
Categorization
? Off-The-Shelf (OTS): Accessories are already available in the market and retailed as supplier
branded.
? Ceer Designed (BTP): Accessories are fully designed by CEER and manufactured as BTP by
suppliers.
? Supplier Designed (FSS): Accessories are designed/modified by supplier with specific
requirements from CEER.
Requirements
Collaboration throughout development and manufacture is critical for ensuring the quality of automotive
accessories for Ceer Motors Company.
Accessory parts suppliers and service providers must be ISO9001 or equivalent certified, with IATF16949
certification being mandatory depending on the nature of the accessories, as agreed with Ceer SQS.
Testing and certification, whether in-house or third-party, are required to validate performance and safety.
Fit and compatibility are necessary, enabling seamless integration without alterations. Robust supplier
quality management systems must be in place, encompassing selection, inspection, and control procedures.
Traceability of materials and components plays an essential role in quality assurance and compliance. Clear
labeling on packaging is required to secure it during travel and installation. Continuous improvement
activities drive enhancements to product design and production processes. This comprehensive strategy
guarantees that accessories meet Ceer requirements and fosters long-term cooperation.
